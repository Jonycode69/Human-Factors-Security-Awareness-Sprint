1. Human Factors in Cybersecurity – Security Awareness Sprint
   ## 1. Project Overview
   This project was completed as part of a two-week cybersecurity internship sprint focused on Human Factors in Cybersecurity.

   The objective was to investigate how human behaviour contributes to cybersecurity incidents, identify common human-factor threats affecting the telecommunications industry, and develop a security awareness campaign to educate employees and everyday users about recognising and preventing cyber threats.

   The project combined cybersecurity research, real-world incident analysis, creative awareness content development, and accessibility considerations.
   ## 2. Project Objectives
   The main objectives were to:
   - Identify and research human-factor threats affecting the telecommunications industry.
   - Investigate real-world cybersecurity incidents involving human error or social engineering.
   - Analyse the impact of human-related security incidents on organisations and stakeholders.
   - Develop an engaging security awareness campaign using the Vivida platform.
   - Create relatable educational content that encourages safer online behaviour.
   - Explore accessibility in cybersecurity and ways to make awareness materials inclusive.
   ## 3. Research: Human Factor Threats
   Human factors refer to the behaviours, decisions, actions, and mistakes of individuals that can influence an organisation's cybersecurity posture.

   During the sprint, we researched several threats, including:
   ### Social Engineering
   Attackers manipulate individuals into revealing sensitive information, granting unauthorised access, or performing actions that compromise security.
   ### Phishing
   Fraudulent emails or messages designed to trick recipients into clicking malicious links, opening harmful attachments, or disclosing confidential information.
   ### Smishing
   SMS-based phishing attacks that use deceptive messages, fake promotions, or urgent notifications to direct victims to malicious websites.
   ### Vishing
   Voice phishing attacks in which attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organisations over phone calls to manipulate victims into taking unsafe actions.
   ### Business Email Compromise (BEC)
   A form of email fraud in which attackers impersonate executives, employees, or trusted business partners to manipulate financial transactions or obtain sensitive information.
   ### Impersonation and Insider Risks
   Attackers exploit trust and authority by pretending to be legitimate employees, managers, technical support personnel, or service providers.
   ## 4. Case Study Research
   The sprint involved researching cybersecurity incidents affecting organisations within or similar to the telecommunications industry.

   The research focused on:
   - Identifying the human factors that contributed to security incidents.
   - Understanding how attackers exploited trust, authority, urgency, or inadequate awareness.
   - Evaluating the impact on organisations, customers, and other stakeholders.
   - Examining organisational responses and identifying preventive measures.
   The case study research reinforced the importance of employee awareness, identity verification, incident reporting, and strong organisational security policies.
   ## 5. Security Awareness Campaign Development
   We collaborated on developing a security awareness campaign using the Vivida platform.

   The campaign focused on making cybersecurity education engaging, practical, and relatable, particularly within the Nigerian telecommunications context.
   ### Campaign Content
   The content developed included:

   **A. Animated Awareness Reels**

   We developed short 3D animated scenarios illustrating common social engineering attacks.

   Examples included:
   - Vishing: An attacker impersonates a trusted corporate representative and attempts to manipulate an employee into granting access to sensitive company information or administrative privileges.
   - Smishing: A fraudulent SMS promises free unlimited mobile data for 30 days, encouraging the recipient to click a suspicious link.
   - Fake Promotions: A relatable scenario demonstrating how attractive offers can influence users to make unsafe online decisions.
   The scenarios highlighted how attacks happen, the mistakes victims make, and the safer actions they should take.

   **B. Image-Based Awareness Posts**

   We developed visual awareness content communicating important cybersecurity messages in a simple and engaging format.

   **C. Text-Based Awareness Posts**

   Short educational messages were created to reinforce good security practices and encourage users to think before clicking, sharing information, or responding to unexpected requests.
   ## 6. Accessibility in Cybersecurity Awareness
   An important part of the sprint was understanding accessibility and its role in cybersecurity education.

   Awareness materials should be understandable and usable by people with different abilities.

   Accessibility considerations included:
   - Adding captions to video content for people with hearing impairments.
   - Providing alternative text for meaningful images.
   - Maintaining sufficient colour contrast between text and backgrounds.
   - Using readable fonts and appropriate text sizes.
   - Avoiding overly complicated language.
   - Ensuring that important security information is not communicated through colour alone.
   - Considering screen-reader compatibility and accessible content formats.
   These considerations help ensure that cybersecurity awareness information reaches a wider audience.
   ## 7. Key Findings and Lessons Learned
   The sprint highlighted several important lessons:
   1. Human behaviour is an important part of an organisation's cybersecurity defence.
   2. Attackers frequently exploit trust, urgency, curiosity, fear, and attractive offers.
   3. Technical security controls alone cannot eliminate risks associated with human behaviour.
2. Continuous security awareness training helps individuals recognise and respond to suspicious activities.
3. Cybersecurity education is more effective when practical examples and relatable scenarios are used.
4. Accessibility should be considered when designing cybersecurity awareness materials.

## 8. Challenges and Approaches

Some of the challenges encountered during the sprint included:

- Translating technical cybersecurity concepts into simple language that everyday users could understand.
- Developing realistic scenarios that reflect common social engineering techniques.
- Making awareness content engaging without losing its educational purpose.
- Considering accessibility while designing visual and video-based content.

These challenges were addressed through research, team collaboration, scenario development, and a focus on practical examples.

## 9. Conclusion

The Human Factors Security Awareness Sprint provided practical experience in researching social engineering threats, analysing security incidents, developing awareness campaigns, and understanding accessibility in cybersecurity.

The project reinforced that cybersecurity is not solely a technical responsibility. Individuals also play an important role in protecting organisational systems, information, and digital services.

Building a strong security culture requires continuous education, awareness, and the ability to recognise and respond appropriately to potential threats.
